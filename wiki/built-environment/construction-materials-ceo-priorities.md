# Construction and Materials: CEO Priorities and Decarbonisation Levers

> Sources: Accenture and United Nations Global Compact, 12th CEO Study industry fact sheet, undated
> Raw: [United Nations Global Compact-Accenture CEO Study: Construction & Materials Industry Insights](../../raw/built-environment/accenture-construction-materials-ceo-study.md)
> Updated: 2026-11-10

## Overview

This one-page Accenture fact sheet, drawn from the 12th United Nations Global Compact-Accenture CEO Study, profiles how construction and materials chief executives are responding to supply-chain disruption, talent scarcity and climate pressure. The industry is described as one of the greatest consumers of raw materials globally and a major carbon emitter, with material shortages and price volatility, coupled with stakeholder pressure, driving a sustainable revolution in business models, materials and building design.

The sheet's headline numbers are that 77 per cent of construction and materials CEOs are upskilling or reskilling their workforce, 71 per cent are digitizing business processes, and 63 per cent are strengthening scenario planning and analysis. On sustainability, 59 per cent are investing in skills development, 56 per cent are introducing new sustainable business models, and 55 per cent are lowering GHG emissions.

## The pressures CEOs are responding to

The fact sheet names three most pressing challenges: inflation and price volatility, talent scarcity, and threats to public health. It presents them as a comparison between construction and materials and cross-industry responses, though the printed chart does not label which percentage belongs to which challenge.

The narrative links the first two. Supply-chain disruptions have caused significant delays and backorders, pushing CEOs to invest in digital tools that improve supply-chain visibility, scenario planning and preparation for inflation and price volatility. At the same time, talent shortages linked to the pandemic are hindering recovery. Companies are also responding to pandemic-driven changes in ways of working by building resilient cultures, fostering employee well-being and adapting to hybrid work.

## Resilience actions

| Action | Share of construction and materials CEOs |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Upskilling or reskilling the workforce for the future labor market | 77% |
| Digitizing business processes | 71% |
| Strengthening scenario planning and analysis capabilities | 63% |

The pattern is consistent: the top actions combine workforce capability with digital and planning capability, rather than treating technology as a standalone fix.

## Sustainability priorities

| Priority | Share of construction and materials CEOs |
|----------|------------------------------------------|
| Investing in skills development | 59% |
| Introducing new sustainable business models | 56% |
| Lowering GHG emissions | 55% |

The fact sheet cites the United Nations Environment Programme's estimate that the built environment accounts for 37% of CO2 emissions. It says construction and materials CEOs are responding by investing in green alternatives to basic materials such as aluminum, steel and concrete.

Digital twins are highlighted as a way to improve resource efficiency — from energy to water usage — across the entire lifecycle of infrastructure. The sheet also points to a modular component trend: components designed to be disassembled, adapted or reused for renovations or new buildings support a more circular model.

> "We need to demonstrate that our industry has a future in a carbon neutral or low carbon economy, which is a tremendous challenge"

— Fernando Gonzalez, Chief Executive Officer of CEMEX

## Transformative innovations

The fact sheet lists five innovations with percentages as printed. It does not state the survey question behind the percentages, so they are best read as relative signals rather than as adoption rates or impact scores.

| Innovation | Percentage |
|------------|------------|
| Innovative low-carbon materials | 62% |
| Distributed renewable energy generation | 58% |
| Use of modular components designed for circularity | 48% |
| Water conservation appliances and systems | 45% |
| Sensors and analytics to monitor building occupancy for operational efficiency | 44% |

The sheet carries a high-impact, moderate-impact, low-impact, no-impact legend but does not assign a category to each innovation in its text.

## Key takeaways

- **The transition is pressure-driven.** Material shortages, price volatility and stakeholder expectations are forcing business-model change, not just greener marketing.
- **Resilience and sustainability overlap on skills.** Workforce development is the top resilience action (77%) and the top sustainability priority (59%), making talent a decarbonisation lever.
- **Digital capability is the connective tissue.** Supply-chain visibility, scenario planning, digital twins and occupancy sensors all depend on better data and processes.
- **Circularity is moving into components.** Modular design for disassembly, adaptation and reuse is presented alongside low-carbon materials and renewable energy as a core innovation.
- **The headline carbon figure is a scope statement.** The 37% share attributed to the built environment sets the scale of the industry's footprint; the sheet does not break that figure down by operational and embodied emissions.
