# Singapore Construction Market Review and Outlook 2026

> Sources: SJ Group, "2026 Singapore Construction Market Review and Outlook", Cost Research Publication #012, May 2026
> Raw: [2026 Singapore Construction Market Review and Outlook](../../raw/built-environment/2026-05-singapore-construction-market-review-and-outlook.md)
> Updated: 2026-11-09

## Overview

SJ Group's May 2026 review records a strong 2025 for Singapore's construction sector and a more uncertain 2026. Total construction demand reached **S$50.5 billion** in 2025, up **13.2 per cent** from 2024, while real sectoral growth was **5.2 per cent**. The Building and Construction Authority (BCA) forecasts 2026 demand at **S$47 billion to S$53 billion**. The outlook is shaped by a Middle East conflict and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, higher energy and material costs, a tight labour market, and a public-sector push on productivity and fair risk-sharing.

## Macroeconomic backdrop

The report's macro scenario is dominated by the conflict that began on **28 February 2026**, when the United States and Israel launched joint aerial strikes against Iran. Iran retaliated against neighbouring Gulf states and closed the **Strait of Hormuz**, a **20-mile-wide** chokepoint that carries about **20 per cent of global oil and gas consumption**.

Singapore is directly exposed. About **70 to 75 per cent** of its crude oil imports originate from the Middle East, and **35 to 40 per cent** of total energy imports – including oil and LNG – pass through the Strait. The conflict was compounded by a **15% global tariff** announced by US President Trump days before hostilities began.

Other macro points in the report:

- Global GDP growth is projected at **3.3 per cent** in 2026, but the IMF warns a sustained conflict could disrupt energy markets and impede growth.
- Singapore's energy and chemicals industry contributes about **2 to 3 per cent** of GDP and around **23 per cent** of total manufacturing output.
- Singapore's economy grew **5.0 per cent** in 2025, driven mainly by AI-related semiconductors and electronics.
- Before the conflict, the 2026 GDP growth forecast was revised from **1 to 3 per cent** to **2 to 4 per cent**.
- MAS forecasts core and average inflation of **1 to 2 percent** for 2026.

## 2025 construction demand

The 2025 performance was supported by public-sector demand, which rose **27 per cent**. Major awards included foundation and tunnelling works for Changi Airport Terminal 5, Toa Payoh Integrated Development, the Eastern Integrated Health Campus, and Alexandra Integrated Hospital Redevelopment.

The land and residential market also contributed:

- **Sixteen** GLS sites were awarded, along with **three** EC sites and **one** mixed-use site at Chencharu Close.
- The maiden GLS site in Bukit Timah Turf City drew **nine** bids.
- Private home prices rose **3.4 per cent**, and new home sales reached their highest level since 2021.
- HDB completed about **19,600** flats across **28** housing projects.
- HDB resale price growth slowed to **2.9 per cent**, down from **9.7 per cent** in 2024.
- The High Court approved the **S$810 million** sale of Thomson View condominium.
- Total investment sales reached **S$34.12 billion**, up **27 per cent**, according to Savills Singapore. Deals included the one-third sale of Marina Bay Financial Centre Tower 3 and the **S$809 million** acquisition of The Clementi Mall.

### Residential

Public residential demand is expected to ease from the 2025 peak to an upper bound of **S$6.8 billion** in 2026. HDB plans to launch approximately **19,600** Build-To-Order flats in 2026 and remains on track to deliver about **55,000** flats from 2025 to 2027.

Private residential demand is expected to moderate slightly to **S$5.5 billion**. The H1 2026 GLS programme includes **fourteen** confirmed-list parcels and **six** reserve-list parcels, expected to yield more than **9,000** units. HDB also launched **three** EC sites.

The collective-sale market remains cautious. Loyang Valley Condominium was acquired for **S$880 million**, the largest residential collective sale since the **S$810 million** Thomson View deal in 2025. The freehold 15-storey Tan Boon Liat Building was relisted at **S$1 billion**, down from **S$1.15 billion** when first launched in February 2025.

Policy changes are also in play. Industry stakeholders have called for the collective-sale consent threshold to be cut from **80 per cent** to **70 per cent**. From **1 March 2026**, Land Betterment Charge rates for non-landed residential use rose **4.1 per cent** – the sharpest rise since September 2022 – while commercial rates rose **0.5 per cent**.

### Commercial

Commercial construction demand fell to **S$2.2 billion** in 2025, about **40 per cent** below earlier projections. It is forecast to recover to **S$6.1 billion to S$6.7 billion** in 2026, helped by the Marina Bay Sands expansion, HarbourFront Centre and Tanglin Shopping Centre.

- **Marina Bay Sands expansion:** completion targeted for **June 2030**, with an official opening expected in **January 2031**. The expansion includes a **55-storey** all-suite hotel tower with **570** suites, approximately **200,000 square feet** of meeting space, and a **15,000-seat** entertainment arena.
- **HarbourFront Centre:** slated for closure and demolition in the second half of 2026, then redeveloped into a **33-storey** tower with **123,000 square metres** of GFA, including **26** levels of Grade A offices and **five** storeys of retail.
- **Tanglin Shopping Centre:** a freehold **68,512 square feet** site with a gross plot ratio of **4.2** and a height control of up to **20 storeys**. Adjacent developments are slated for a **1.23 million square feet** mixed-use project.
- **Hotels:** a **200-key** Avani property has broken ground at the former Nehsons Building, and Therme Group is developing a wellness attraction in Marina South targeted for completion in **2030**.
- **Retail:** the rear block of The Centrepoint was sold for **S$391.9 million**, the largest collective sale so far in 2026. Cuppage Terrace, with **17** adjoining Peranakan conservation shophouses, was launched for sale.
- **Offices:** two towers at 78 Shenton Way changed hands; the **56-year** leasehold property may gain up to **30%** more space for residential use under the CBD Incentive Scheme.

### Industrial

Industrial construction demand was **S$7.1 billion** in 2025 and is expected to moderate to **S$4.6 billion to S$5.4 billion** in 2026. The manufacturing sector grew **15 per cent** year-on-year in Q4 2025, and factory activity rose to **50.5** in January, a **10-month** high.

Industrial property remained tight: prices rose **5 per cent** and rents **2.4 per cent**. **Six** single-user sites and **four** multiple-user sites were awarded under the industrial GLS programme.

Investment commitments were strong:

- Fixed Asset Investment commitments reached **S$14.2 billion** in 2025, **5.3 per cent** higher than 2024. More than a third were in electronics, and about **S$12.1 billion** were manufacturing-related.
- Micron Technology began an advanced wafer fabrication facility representing a planned **S$31 billion** investment over **10 years**, with **700,000 square feet** of cleanroom space and wafer output scheduled for H2 2028.
- The government launched a second Data Centre Call for Application, making at least **200MW** of new capacity available. Singapore also plans a **700MW** low-carbon data centre park on Jurong Island across approximately **20 hectares**, which could raise total capacity by nearly **50 per cent** from a current live capacity of around **1GW**.
- Bridge Data Centres announced plans to invest up to **S$5 billion** in AI technology in Singapore, including a floating hydrogen power generation solution.

### Infrastructure and institutional

Civil engineering demand is expected to strengthen to **S$11.6 billion to S$13.4 billion** in 2026.

- In Q1 2026, the Land Transport Authority awarded more than **S$1.2 billion** in contracts for tunnels and stations under the Downtown Line 2 Extension.
- Changi Airport Group awarded a **S$3.8 billion** substructure contract and a **S$950 million** airside infrastructure contract for Terminal 5. Superstructure, ground transport centre and landside works are out to tender, with awards anticipated in **2026/2027**.
- Phase 1 of the Greater Sentosa Masterplan has begun, including a new transport hub linking Sentosa and Pulau Brani and a replacement for the Sentosa Express monorail.
- Main construction at Alexandra Hospital began in December 2025. MOH awarded an **S$82 million** contract for advance infrastructure works for Singapore General Hospital Phase 2A, and the main contract tender for Tengah General and Community Hospital is in progress.
- MOH also announced a **55,542 square metres** office building within the SGH campus and a **22,800 square metre** site near Paya Lebar MRT station for a health and medical care development.
- Other projects include an **18,000-seater** indoor arena under the Kallang Alive Masterplan and a new Singapore University of Social Sciences city campus at the former Rochor Centre site.

## Tender prices, materials and cost escalation

The BCA Tender Price Index rose **1.01 per cent** year-on-year in 2025. Raw material prices were relatively stable except cement, which rose more than **13 per cent** compared with early 2025, while steel reinforcement prices continued to fall.

The conflict has pushed oil prices above **US$100 per barrel**, their highest level in over two years, and disrupted supply chains. The Singapore Contractors Association Limited observed construction material prices rising by approximately **5 to 15 per cent or more**. Assuming a **20 per cent** price escalation in key materials such as concrete, reinforcement, M&E equipment and architectural finishes, overall construction cost pressures for a typical residential project could rise by around **2 to 4 per cent**. Preliminary cost escalation for 2026 is projected at **2 to 5 per cent**.

The report cautions that a prolonged conflict, or a sustained oil crisis with rising inflation and slower growth, could require construction demand to be recalibrated to a softer macroeconomic outlook.

## Labour and industry response

The construction sector's Dependency Ratio Ceiling remains at **83.3 per cent**, equivalent to one local employee for every **five** Work Permit holders. Foreign Worker Levy rates for construction are unchanged for now, but dormitory rents are expected to rise. Crane demand has increased significantly over the last five years, with rental rates rising by up to **10 per cent**.

The report argues that productivity is essential given the gap between demand and output. It recommends:

- Accelerating AI, digitalisation, design standardisation and modular construction.
- Strengthening project management across time, cost, quality and safety.
- Using collaborative contracting and equitable risk-sharing to manage price volatility and procurement risk.
- Supporting the built environment professions: from **1 December 2025**, government contracts must include a clause limiting a consultant's contractual liability, the Reduced Fee Score now covers public-sector projects up to **S$100 million**, and BCA has set up a workgroup to strengthen quantity surveying.

## Key takeaways

- **2025 was strong, but 2026 is a pause rather than a collapse.** Demand is forecast at S$47 billion to S$53 billion, straddling 2025's S$50.5 billion and still historically high.
- **Civil engineering is the clearest growth area**, with 2026 demand projected at S$13.4 billion and 44.09% growth, driven by Terminal 5, rail extensions and Sentosa works.
- **Commercial demand rebounds sharply** from a weak 2025 base, while residential and industrial decline from 2025 levels.
- **Cost risk is tilted upward.** Energy, cement and imported materials are the main pressure points, with 2026 escalation projected at 2 to 5 per cent.
- **Productivity and risk-sharing are the policy response**, alongside continued reliance on a high Dependency Ratio Ceiling.
