# HDB Demo Day 2026: Built Environment Innovations

> Sources: Isabelle Liew, The Straits Times, 2026-10-28
> Raw: [New material to cope with algae, mould from condensation among innovations unveiled at tech showcase](../../raw/built-environment/2026-10-28-hdb-demo-day-innovations.md)
> Updated: 2026-11-10

## Overview

HDB and Enterprise Singapore held their first Demo Day on Oct 28, 2026, showcasing 13 innovations at the Singapore Week of Innovation and Technology 2026 in the Sands Expo and Convention Centre. The event links enterprises with developers and building contractors to explore partnership and deployment opportunities, and the innovations aim to address challenges in construction productivity, ageing infrastructure and urban heat resilience.

The article leads with an anti-condensation coating being trialled in HDB flats, and also describes a remote construction coordination platform, a protective coating for refuse chutes and microwave scanning technology for concrete defects. Enterprise Singapore committed $4 million to the HDB Cool Ideas Enterprise programme, bringing its funding to $9 million, and HDB opened the Building Research, Innovation and Collaboration Lab.

## The condensation and mould problem

In 2025, HDB received 240 cases of feedback involving condensation and mould on ceilings and walls arising from use of air-conditioners by neighbours. The problem occurs when a temperature difference causes moisture to condense on indoor walls.

Envicom's anti-condensation coating is a type of plaster applied to the wall. Leonard Lim, managing director at Envicom, said it traps the cold inside the wall – similar to a cooler box – and prevents condensation from forming. The coating is being used in HDB flats where algae and mould began growing on the walls because of the temperature difference when the air-conditioner is turned on in the neighbouring flat.

## Innovations showcased

| Innovation | Developer | What it does |
|------------|-----------|--------------|
| Anti-condensation coating | Envicom | A plaster applied to walls that traps cold inside the wall and prevents condensation, reducing algae and mould caused by a neighbour's air-conditioner. |
| Remote construction coordination platform | QPBuddy | Lets contractors, site supervisors and engineers coordinate construction activities remotely and monitor on-site tests in real time. |
| Protective coating for refuse chutes | Imerys | Reduces corrosion and deterioration of HDB refuse-chute walls. |
| Microwave scanning technology | WaveScan | Scans beneath concrete surfaces without hacking or drilling and can identify issues such as concrete spalling years before they become visible or dangerous. |

The 13 innovations were not all described in the article; the four above are the solutions it details.

## WaveScan pilot and water seepage

WaveScan's microwave scanning technology has been rolled out in a pilot scheme in HDB flats. Between 2024 and 2025, HDB carried out trials in more than 60 flats with water seepage issues, and also deployed the technology for complex water seepage cases, according to Minister of State for National Development Alvin Tan.

## Funding and research infrastructure

In his speech at the event, Alvin Tan said Enterprise Singapore has committed $4 million to support the development of innovative solutions to improve the built environment under the HDB Cool Ideas Enterprise programme. That brings the programme's funding to $9 million.

Tan added that HDB is looking for proposals to address challenges in construction productivity, ageing infrastructure and heat resilience, and that the solutions could be tried out in HDB housing projects.

HDB also opened the Building Research, Innovation and Collaboration Lab on Oct 28. The lab is a co-working space at the HDB Centre of Building Research in Woodlands, where innovators have access to facilities and work alongside HDB's engineers, researchers and industry partners. Tan said this would help companies bring their solutions closer to actual deployment.

## Perspectives

> "This is a good example of how innovation can solve real problems for residents, improve how we maintain our ageing infrastructure, and create opportunities for our enterprises," he added.

— Alvin Tan, Minister of State for National Development

> "By sharing our technical expertise and testing facilities with enterprises, we can help promising innovations move beyond the laboratory and accelerate their adoption in the real world and in public housing projects."

— Johnny Wong, HDB deputy chief executive for building

Johnny Wong described HDB as Singapore's largest housing developer and said it is always exploring innovation that can improve construction productivity and better maintain ageing infrastructure.

## Key takeaways

- **Public housing as a testbed.** HDB is using its flats and housing projects to trial innovations, with the aim of moving promising solutions from the laboratory into real-world deployment.
- **Problem-led innovation.** The showcased solutions respond to specific operational problems: condensation and mould, water seepage, refuse-chute corrosion and remote construction coordination.
- **Funding bridges the gap.** The HDB Cool Ideas Enterprise programme now has $9 million in funding, and Demo Day connects enterprises with developers and contractors that can deploy their solutions.
- **Ageing infrastructure is a central challenge.** WaveScan's scanning technology and the new research lab are both positioned around maintaining Singapore's ageing building stock.
- **Urban heat resilience is a named priority.** It is one of the three challenge areas HDB is seeking proposals for, although the article does not describe specific heat-resilience solutions.
