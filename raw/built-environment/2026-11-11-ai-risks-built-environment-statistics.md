# AI Adoption Risks in the Built Environment: Compiled Statistics

> Source: compiled during research on the biggest risks of adopting AI in the built environment, drawing on the published sources listed inline below. Unlike other raw files in this collection, this is a synthesis of many sources rather than a single article; every statistic is attributed to its original publisher and linked.
> Collected: 2026-11-11

Research question: which risks of adopting AI in the built environment are the biggest, with quantitative support? Four candidate risks were shortlisted before the research: poor-quality outputs from poor-quality data inputs; lack of AI governance policies; lack of staff knowledge on using and supervising AI; and low or no ROI from adopting inappropriate (hype-driven) tools. The statistics below are grouped to test each, plus additional risks surfaced by the research.

A follow-up round of research the same day asked which steps mitigate each of the four risks, with quantitative evidence that the step works. Those statistics are compiled in section 12, and each mitigation is supported either by direct outcome evidence (experiments, measured before/after differences, breach costs) or by causal-chain evidence (the risk statistics above identify a cause; the mitigation removes it).

## 1. Surveys asking stakeholders to rank AI risks and barriers

### RICS, "Artificial intelligence in construction report 2025" (2,200+ professionals worldwide, ~48% UK)

Source: https://www.rics.org/news-insights/artificial-intelligence-in-construction-report

Respondents chose their top three challenges to AI adoption:

- **46%** — lack of skilled personnel (the most cited barrier)
- **37%** — integration with existing systems
- **30%** — data quality and availability
- **29%** — high implementation costs
- **28%** — unclear return on investment
- **22%** — privacy and security concerns
- **11%** — regulatory or legal uncertainty

Adoption maturity context: **45%** reported no AI implementation at their organisation; **34%** were in early pilots; just under **12%** reported regular use of AI in specific processes; **1.5%** used it across multiple processes; fewer than **1%** had AI embedded organisation-wide. **44%** of project managers and **38%** of quantity surveying and construction professionals were concerned about AI's impact on their own role.

### Dodge Construction Network and CMiC, US contractor AI research, December 2025

Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251205015633/en/New-Research-Reveals-Strong-Contractor-Optimism-About-AIs-Transformative-Impact-on-Construction-Industry ; https://www.construction.com/resource/ai-for-contractors/

- **87%** of US contractors believe AI will have a meaningful impact on construction, but only **19%** have adapted their workflows for it
- **57%** are concerned about the accuracy of AI output
- **54%** are concerned about data security
- Only **26%** rate the quality of their existing data as high
- **40%** are allocating a dedicated AI budget; **38%** are creating AI implementation teams
- 20 of the 23 AI-enabled project and company management functions studied are used by fewer than **15%** of US contractors
- **81%** see benefit in automated constructability analysis that finds likely field issues during design

### Bluebeam AEC research, October 2025 (1,000+ AEC professionals, North America, Europe, Australia)

Sources: https://press.bluebeam.com/2025/10/new-bluebeam-report-shows-early-ai-adopters-in-aec-seeing-significant-roi-despite-uneven-adoption/ ; https://www.asce.org/publications-and-news/civil-engineering-source/article/2025/12/18/architecture-engineering-construction-sector-slow-to-adapt-ai-survey-shows

- **42%** cite data-sharing security as an AI integration challenge (the most common answer)
- **33%** cite cost and complexity
- **69%** say concerns about potential AI regulation have affected their AI efforts
- **23%** say it is hard to keep up with rapidly changing technologies
- **52%** still use paper during the design phase and **49%** during planning; **43%** rely on physical signatures and approvals
- **27%** of AEC firms use AI for automation, problem-solving or decision-making; **94%** of those plan to expand AI use
- **68%** of AEC firms already using AI report saving at least $50,000; **46%** report saving 500 to 1,000 hours
- **84%** plan to increase overall technology investment in 2026

### Autodesk, State of Design and Make 2025, construction spotlight

Source: https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/state-of-design-make-spotlight-construction/

- **55%** of construction leaders cite a lack of skilled talent as a barrier to growth, up from **43%** a year earlier
- **68%** believe AI will enhance the construction industry, down from **80%** the year before

### Eide Bailly / Construction Dive, Construction Industry Outlook (business leaders)

Source: https://www.eidebailly.com/insights/tools/construction-dive-industry-report

Four primary struggles with AI adoption:

- **45%** cited a lack of clarity on where to begin
- **21%** had concerns related to security and governance
- **18%** were uncertain of AI's value
- **17%** faced challenges with data quality

### Pinsent Masons, Bentley Systems, Mott MacDonald and Turner & Townsend global survey (86 respondents, 75% C-suite/board, Feb–June 2025)

Source: raw/built-environment/2025-10-31-ai-adoption-challenge-built-environment.md (already in collection)

- **60%** of organisations had some kind of AI policy; only **20%** had an organisation-wide policy covering use, governance, ethics and safety; **6%** department-specific policies; **12%** none
- **37%** had limited or no project controls to manage risk where AI is used for design and construction
- Adoption described as "gold rush fever"; risks identified: health and safety (plausible but incorrect results producing flawed risk assessments), supply-chain cyber risk, data quality and security, bias and opacity, erosion of human oversight

## 2. ROI and project-failure outcome data

### MIT Project NANDA, "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025" (July/August 2025)

Sources: https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo ; https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf

- Despite **$30–40 billion** of enterprise GenAI investment, **95%** of organisations saw zero measurable P&L return; only **5%** of integrated pilots extracted millions in value
- Based on executive interviews and analysis of **300+** public AI deployments
- The divide "does not seem to be driven by model quality or regulation" but by approach: learning gap, workflow integration, absence of a defined outcome before build
- Purchasing AI tools from specialised vendors and partnerships succeed about **67%** of the time; internal builds succeed only about one-third as often
- Employees drive a "shadow AI economy", using consumer tools without employer approval

### Gartner

Sources: https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025 ; https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/genai-project-failure ; https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-02-26-lack-of-ai-ready-data-puts-ai-projects-at-risk ; https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027

- July 2024 prediction: at least **30%** of GenAI projects abandoned after proof of concept by end of 2025, due to poor data quality, inadequate risk controls, escalating costs or unclear business value
- January 2026 (Arun Chandrasekaran, from analysis of hundreds of GenAI implementations): at least **50%** of GenAI projects were abandoned after proof of concept by the end of 2025; failure point No. 1 is lack of business value
- February 2025: through 2026, organisations will abandon **60%** of AI projects unsupported by AI-ready data; **63%** of organisations either lack or are unsure they have the right data management practices for AI (survey of 1,203 data management leaders, July 2024)
- June 2025: over **40%** of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027; most agentic AI projects are "early stage experiments or proof of concepts that are mostly driven by hype and are often misapplied"

### S&P Global Market Intelligence, survey of 1,000+ firms (reported March 2025)

Source: via https://sipoly.si/claim/gartner-genai-30pct-abandoned-2025 (evidence timeline)

- The average organisation scrapped **46%** of AI proofs-of-concept before production
- **42%** of companies abandoned most of their AI initiatives

### McKinsey, "The state of AI" (November 2025 and 2026 updates)

Sources: https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025 ; https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai

- **88%** of organisations use AI in at least one function (up from 78%)
- Only **39%** report any enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI, and most of those say less than 5% of EBIT is attributable to AI; the 2026 update holds at **37%**
- AI "high performers" (≥5% EBIT impact and significant value) are about **6%** of respondents, unchanged year on year

### RIBA AI Report 2026 (UK architecture practices, fieldwork March–April 2026)

Sources: https://www.riba.org/work/insights-and-resources/ai-report ; https://beale-law.com/article/riba-ai-report-2026-charts-the-growing-role-of-artificial-intelligence-in-architectural-practice ; https://www.archipractica.ai/data/how-architects-use-ai

- **74%** of practices use AI on at least some projects (41% in 2024, 59% in 2025); only **15%** say AI is embedded in day-to-day work
- **73%** of AI users report improved productivity; **57%** of those assessing ROI report a positive return
- But only **17%** agree their designs are "better" because of AI, and only **7%** say AI has enabled them to increase fees (reported in secondary coverage)

## 3. Data quality

### Gartner

Source: https://www.gartner.com/en/data-analytics/topics/data-quality

- Poor data quality costs organisations an average of **$12.9 million** per year (2020 research, still cited by Gartner)
- **59%** of organisations do not measure data quality

### Autodesk and FMI, bad-data research (2021)

Sources: https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/construction-industry-statistics ; https://fmicorp.com/insights/thought-leadership/beyond-the-buzz-harnessing-the-power-of-data-analytics-in-construction

- Bad data may have cost the global construction industry **$1.8–1.85 trillion** in 2020
- Bad data was potentially responsible for **14%** of avoidable rework, amounting to **$88.69 billion**
- **30%** of construction professionals say more than half their project data is bad (inaccurate, incomplete, inconsistent or untimely)
- European study of 1,115 professionals: on average **40%** of an organisation's data is bad

### FMI, "Big Data = Big Questions" (2018)

Source: https://fmicorp.com/insights/thought-leadership/beyond-the-buzz-harnessing-the-power-of-data-analytics-in-construction

- **95–96%** of all data captured in engineering and construction goes unused
- **13%** of E&C working hours are spent looking for project data and information
- **30%** of E&C companies use applications that do not integrate with one another; only **8%** have real-time project management information systems

### PlanGrid and FMI, "Construction Disconnected" (2018, ~600 professionals)

Sources: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/new-research-from-plangrid-and-fmi-identifies-factors-costing-the-construction-industry-more-than-177-billion-annually-300689826.html ; https://pg.plangrid.com/rs/572-JSV-775/images/Construction_Disconnected.pdf

- **52%** of rework worldwide is caused by poor project data and miscommunication (~$280 billion globally; $31.3 billion in the US in 2018, 48% of US rework)
- **26%** of rework is attributed to poor communication; **22%** to poor project information
- Construction professionals spend **35%** of their working hours (~14 hours a week) looking for project data, resolving conflicts and dealing with mistakes and rework

### Dodge (Smartapp study) — integration

Source: https://www.construction.com/new-study-greater-automation-improves-general-contractors-projects

- **76%** of general contractors report data integration challenges, with impacts on analytics, budget control, cost predictability, profitability and delivery dates

Source: https://pacific.ai/2025-ai-governance-survey
- **PEX Report 2025/26**: only **43%** of businesses have an AI governance policy (while 63% use generative AI). Source: https://www.aidataanalytics.network/data-science-ai/news-trends/less-than-half-of-businesses-have-an-ai-governance-policy
- **EY survey (2025)**: nearly half of organisations lack formal AI usage policies; **80%** still need to develop AI risk management controls; fewer than one in three have clear roles, responsibilities and oversight. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aradhnaganvir_ai-governance-in-2025-what-organizations-activity-7413882095154073601-doh6

- **IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2026**: only **32%** of breached organisations have AI policies in place (down from 37%); just **19%** coordinate between AI governance and security teams. Source: https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/ibm-2026-breach-report-ai-governance
- **Deloitte State of AI 2026**: **74%** of organisations plan to deploy AI agents within two years; just **21%** have a mature governance model for them. Source: https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/every-company-needs-an-ai-use-policy-4019321

## 5. Skills, training and supervision

- **RICS 2025**: lack of skilled personnel is the most cited AI adoption barrier (**46%**)
- **Autodesk 2025**: **55%** of construction leaders cite lack of skilled talent as a barrier to growth (up from 43%)
- **Dodge + CMiC 2025**: **87%** believe AI will have a meaningful impact but only **19%** have adapted workflows
- **Bluebeam 2025**: **23%** say it is hard to keep up with rapidly changing technologies
- **Eide Bailly**: **45%** of leaders lack clarity on where to begin with AI
- **RIBA AI Report 2026**: **61%** of respondents believe AI will make it harder for early-career professionals to gain the skills they need, because the routine tasks susceptible to automation are how junior architects build experience
- **Microsoft Research and Carnegie Mellon University, CHI 2025** ("The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking", survey of 319 knowledge workers, 936 real use examples): higher confidence in GenAI is associated with **less** critical thinking (β = −0.69, p < 0.001); higher self-confidence is associated with more critical thinking; GenAI shifts critical thinking from information gathering and problem-solving toward verification, integration and stewardship. Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking-self-reported-reductions-in-cognitive-effort-and-confidence-effects-from-a-survey-of-knowledge-workers
- **MIT Media Lab, "Your Brain on ChatGPT" (2025)**, 54 participants writing SAT essays across LLM, search-engine and brain-only groups with EEG: brain connectivity scaled down with external support — LLM users showed the weakest connectivity; when LLM users later wrote unaided, they still showed reduced alpha and beta connectivity ("cognitive debt"); LLM users had the lowest recall of and ownership over their own essays. Source: https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/your-brain-on-chatgpt

## 6. Confidentiality, shadow AI and cybersecurity

### Data leakage into AI tools

- **Cyberhaven (2025, telemetry across millions of workers)**: the share of corporate data employees put into AI tools that is sensitive more than tripled in two years, from **10.7%** to **34.8%**; **39.7%** of all AI interactions involve sensitive data; corporate data placed into AI tools rose **485%** from March 2023 to March 2024; **32.3%** of ChatGPT usage occurs through personal accounts (Claude 58.2%, Perplexity 60.9%); **71.7%** of 700+ AI tools reviewed fall into high or critical risk categories. Sources: https://www.cyberhaven.com/blog/sensitive-data-flowing-into-ai-tools ; https://www.cfodive.com/news/shadow-it-surge-threatens-corporate-data-report/716686 ; https://www.cyberhaven.com/blog/ai-at-work-is-exploding-but-71-of-tools-put-your-data-at-risk
- **LayerX Security (2025)**: **77%** of employees who use AI have pasted company data into ChatGPT-type tools; **82%** of those pastes come from personal or unmanaged accounts. Source: https://unio.digital/chatgpt-sensitive-data
- **IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025** (600+ organisations): **20%** of organisations (one in five) experienced breaches linked to shadow AI; high levels of shadow AI added about **$670,000** to the average breach cost; shadow-AI breaches disproportionately exposed customer PII (65%) and intellectual property (40%), with IP carrying the highest cost per record (USD 178). Sources: https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/data-matters/cost-of-a-data-breach ; https://www.ibm.com/think/x-force/2025-cost-of-a-data-breach-navigating-ai
- **IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2026**: shadow-AI incidents more than doubled, from **20%** to **43%** of breached organisations, with average costs of **$5.39 million**; roughly one in five triggered regulatory fines. Source: https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/ibm-2026-breach-report-ai-governance

### Construction cyber exposure

- **NordLocker**: construction ranked the **#1 most targeted industry** by cyberattacks. Source: https://www.corvusinsurance.com/resources/cybersecurity-101/cyber-and-construction-laying-groundwork-to-combat-digital-threats
- **Corvus (2024)**: ransomware attacks on construction increased **48%** from 2022 to 2023
- **Dodge + Egnyte (2023)**: **59%** of firms experienced a cybersecurity threat within the previous two years
- **Travelers survey**: only **20%** of construction decision-makers say they are knowledgeable about cyber issues that could harm their companies; only **30%** have a business continuity plan
- **RICS 2025**: **22%** list privacy and security concerns among their top AI barriers
- **Dodge + CMiC 2025**: **54%** of US contractors concerned about data security in AI

## 7. Accuracy and hallucination

- **Dodge + CMiC 2025**: **57%** of US contractors are concerned about the accuracy of AI output
- **Stanford RegLab/HAI — Dahl et al. 2024 (Journal of Legal Analysis)**: general-purpose LLMs hallucinate **58–88%** of the time on specific, verifiable legal queries (GPT-4 58%, GPT-3.5 69%, Llama 2 88%). Source: https://hai.stanford.edu/news/ai-trial-legal-models-hallucinate-1-out-6-or-more-benchmarking-queries
- **Stanford — Magesh et al. 2025 (Journal of Empirical Legal Studies)**: purpose-built legal research tools (Lexis+ AI, Westlaw AI-Assisted Research, Ask Practical Law) still hallucinate **17–33%** of the time. Source: https://reglab.stanford.edu/publications/hallucination-free-assessing-the-reliability-of-leading-ai-legal-research-tools
- **Discover Artificial Intelligence (2025), FE civil exam study**: GPT-4 answered **76.6%** and GPT-3.5 **51.1%** of text-only Fundamentals of Engineering (civil) exam questions correctly — roughly one in four to one in two questions wrong — and the models were unlikely to pass the FE exam because of image-based questions. Source: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44163-025-00646-6
- **BBC research (October 2025)**: **45%** of AI answers to news-related queries had significant issues (wrong facts, wrong or distorted dates, sources not supporting claims). Source: https://joshbersin.com/2025/10/bbc-finds-that-45-of-ai-queries-produce-erroneous-answers
- **RIBA AI Report 2026**: highlights hallucination and unreliable outputs, data security, privacy and professional insurance as key risks; AI use for standards and regulatory compliance checking is now reported by **40%** of AI users. Source: https://beale-law.com/article/riba-ai-report-2026-charts-the-growing-role-of-artificial-intelligence-in-architectural-practice
- **RIBA AI Report 2025**: **47%** believe AI allows those without sufficient professional knowledge to design buildings, increasing the risk of buildings being unsafe, unsustainable or not meeting client needs (2026: **55%**). Source: https://www.riba.org/media/uqmlay5m/riba-ai-report-2025-final.pdf

## 8. IP, liability, insurance and regulation

- **RIBA 2025/2026**: **69%** (2025) rising to **76%** (2026) of respondents believe AI increases the risk of their work being imitated; **35%** see AI as a threat to the profession (2025)
- **IBM 2025**: intellectual property exposed in **40%** of shadow-AI breaches, carrying the highest per-record cost (USD 178)

- **EU AI Act**: enforcement began 2 August 2026 for general-purpose AI models, prohibited practices and transparency rules; rules for Annex III high-risk systems apply from 2 December 2027; breaches involving prohibited practices can draw fines of up to **€35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover**, and other breaches up to €15 million or 3%. Sources (via Helonic's compilation citing the European Commission AI Act Service Desk): https://helonic.com/blog/ai-in-construction-statistics
- **Bluebeam 2025**: **69%** say concerns about potential AI regulation have affected their AI efforts
- **RICS 2025**: **11%** list regulatory or legal uncertainty among top AI barriers

Source: https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Future_of_Jobs_Report_2025.pdf

## 12. Mitigation effectiveness (follow-up research, same day)

Research question: which steps mitigate each of the four shortlisted risks, with quantitative evidence that the step works? Two kinds of support appear below: direct outcome evidence (field experiments, measured before/after differences, breach costs) and causal-chain evidence (the risk statistics above identify a cause; the mitigation removes it).

### Data foundations: common data environment, standardization, measurement (risk 1)

- **Autodesk + FMI (2021)**: the **12%** of respondents who said they always incorporate project data into their decision-making employ intentional data strategies — regularly reviewing data for quality (**40%**), standardizing data collection, reporting and monitoring practices (**38%**), and structuring data in a common data environment for centralized access (**38%**) — and these firms reported fewer project delays and budget overruns, less rework, fewer change orders and reduced safety incidents. Source: https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/autodesk-fmi-study-global-construction-industry-data-strategies
- **Autodesk + Dodge**: **58%** of contractors use standardized inputs on at least half of their projects; **41%** agree that non-standardized data input leads to inconsistent, inaccurate, incomplete and unusable data. Source: https://www.autodesk.com/blogs/construction/construction-industry-statistics
- Prerequisites cross-referenced from above: **59%** of organizations do not measure data quality at all (Gartner, section 3); purpose-built grounded (RAG) tools hallucinate **17–33%** of the time on verifiable professional queries versus **58–88%** for general-purpose LLMs (Stanford, section 7).

### Governance: formal strategy, ownership, controls (risk 2)

- **Writer (2025)**: organizations with a formal AI strategy report an **80%** AI adoption success rate versus **37%** for those without — a 43-percentage-point gap. Source: https://evolvancemarketresearch.com/statistics/ai-governance-statistics
- **Thomson Reuters, Future of Professionals Report 2025 (2,275 professionals across legal, tax, risk, compliance, audit and trade)**: organizations with visible AI strategies are **3.5x** as likely to experience critical AI benefits (versus those with no significant adoption plans) and **1.9x (about twice)** as likely to already be experiencing AI-driven revenue growth (versus informal adopters); only **22%** have a visible, defined AI strategy. Sources: https://www.thomsonreuters.com/content/dam/ewp-m/documents/thomsonreuters/en/pdf/reports/future-of-professionals-report-2025.pdf ; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/the-ai-adoption-reality-check-firms-with-ai-strategies-are-twice-as-likely-to-see-ai-driven-revenue-growth-those-without-risk-falling-behind-302490973.html
- **Pacific AI / Gradient Flow, 2025 AI Governance Survey (351 respondents, fielded 14 Feb–29 May 2025; 91% with US operations)**: **75%** of organizations have AI usage policies but only **36%** have a formal governance framework; **59%** have a dedicated governance role or office; **54%** have AI incident-response playbooks; **48%** monitor their production AI systems; only **22%** provide regular AI literacy training. Governance is strongly scale-dependent: small companies report **36%** governance roles, **41%** annual AI training and **9%** production monitoring. The report concludes that the most successful organizations — those managing multiple production deployments — embed monitoring, risk assessment and incident response directly into engineering workflows. Sources: https://pacific.ai/2025-ai-governance-survey (the 36% framework figure via https://www.knostic.ai/blog/ai-governance-statistics)
- **IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2025 (600 breached organizations, Mar 2024–Feb 2025)**: **13%** of organizations reported breaches of AI models or applications, and **97%** of those compromised lacked AI access controls; **63%** of breached organizations lack or are still developing an AI governance policy; of those with policies, only **34%** perform regular audits for unsanctioned AI; **20%** suffered shadow-AI breaches; organizations using security AI and automation extensively saved an average **$1.9 million** in breach costs and shortened the breach lifecycle by **80 days**. Source: https://newsroom.ibm.com/2025-07-30-ibm-report-13-of-organizations-reported-breaches-of-ai-models-or-applications,-97-of-which-reported-lacking-proper-ai-access-controls
- **Cloud Security Alliance (December 2025 research, cited in its May 2026 shadow-AI research note)**: organizations with mature AI governance programs are **2x** as likely to adopt agentic AI successfully and **3x** more likely to train staff on AI security tools; a survey of 12,000 white-collar employees found **60.2%** had used AI at work while only **18.5%** were aware of any official company AI policy — policy existence without active communication changes little. Source: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-shadow-ai-apps-enterprise-20260530-csa-sty

### Skills: training, sanctioned tools, verification (risk 3)

- **Microsoft + LinkedIn, Work Trend Index 2024 (31,000 respondents, 31 countries)**: only **39%** of AI users received company-provided training; "AI power users" — those using AI several times a week and saving more than 30 minutes a day — are **37%** more likely to have a company virtual learning program with AI-focused training, and more likely to have received prompt-writing training (**+37%**), role-specific training (**+35%**) and use-case training (**+32%**). Source: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/ai-at-work-is-here-now-comes-the-hard-part
- **AWS + Access Partnership (employer surveys including 2,000 western European organizations; companion US study)**: employers expect AI-skilled staff to raise productivity by **at least 39%** (western Europe; **47%** in the US study); only **14%** of workers describe their AI skills as advanced, and roughly three-quarters of employers report difficulty hiring AI-skilled staff — training the existing workforce is the only scalable route. Source: https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/apn/make-digital-and-ai-upskilling-your-competitive-advantage
- **LinkedIn Learning, Workplace Learning Report 2025**: organizations strong at career development ("career development champions") are **32%** more likely to be deploying AI training programs this year, and **51%** describe their organization as a frontrunner in generative AI adoption versus **36%** of others. Sources: https://business.linkedin.com/learn/resources/workplace-learning-report ; https://business.linkedin.com/content/dam/lem/business/en/learn/workplace-learning-report-2025/LinkedIn-Workplace-Learning-Report-2025-anz.pdf
- **NCCER, "From Training to Performance: Evaluating the ROI of Craft Worker Development" (Hannah, Harper and Anator, June 2025; company survey data from 2023)**: construction companies that provide craft training achieve productivity estimates about **two-thirds of the time**, versus a little over **50%** for companies that do not; craft workers at companies providing training perform rework less often — **18.9%** versus **21.8%**. Source: https://www.nccer.org/media/2025/06/NCCER-ROI-From-Training-to-Performance-Report_2025.pdf
- **Cloud Security Alliance research note (May 2026), citing Unseen Security, State of Shadow AI 2026**: when organizations provision sanctioned AI tools, unauthorized use drops by **89%** — supply-side governance rather than blocking is the highest-leverage response. Blocking alone fails: **46%** of workers would keep using personal AI tools even if their employer banned them (Software AG survey of 6,000 workers in the US, UK and Germany, 2024). Sources: https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-shadow-ai-apps-enterprise-20260530-csa-sty ; https://dope.security/post/shadow-ai-statistics
- **Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality" (preregistered field experiment with 758 BCG consultants; Organization Science 37(2), March–April 2026; first circulated as HBS Working Paper 24-013, September 2023)**: on 18 tasks inside GPT-4's capability frontier, consultants with AI completed **12.2%** more tasks, about **25%** faster, with quality more than **30%** higher; below-average consultants gained the most (**+43%**, versus +17% for above-average). On a task outside the frontier, AI users were **19 percentage points** less likely to reach the correct answer, with the harm concentrated among consultants who accepted AI output with too little scrutiny. Sources: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=64700 ; https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2023-10/SSRN-id4573321.pdf

### Use-case discipline and buy-versus-build (risk 4)

- **Gartner (January 2026)**: organizations that master the fundamentals — defining business value and metrics before building — turn pilots into production at **twice the rate**; failure point No. 1 is lack of business value. Source: https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/genai-project-failure
- **MIT NANDA (2025)**, cross-referenced from section 2: purchasing AI tools from specialized vendors or partnering with them succeeds about **67%** of the time; internal builds succeed only about a third as often. The report attributes the GenAI divide to approach, not model quality — the absence of a defined outcome before building is a named cause.
- **Bluebeam (2025)**, cross-referenced from section 11: **68%** of AI-using AEC firms report saving at least $50,000; **46%** report saving 500–1,000 hours — evidence that measured, firm-level savings are achievable in the sector.
- **Dodge + CMiC (2025)**, cross-referenced from section 1: **38%** of US contractors are creating dedicated AI implementation teams; **40%** are allocating a dedicated AI budget.
- **ISG (2025, via compiled AI governance statistics)**: **31%** of AI use cases studied reached full production in 2025 — double the 2024 rate. Source: https://evolvancemarketresearch.com/statistics/ai-governance-statistics

## Methodological notes

- Adoption percentages vary by survey because definitions of "use" differ (AGC-Sage counts intent to invest; RICS counts regular use in live processes). Quote the definition with the rate.
- Gartner's 30% and 50% abandonment figures are analyst analyses of "hundreds of GenAI implementations" with no published method; the 60% AI-ready-data figure is a prediction.
- MIT NANDA's 95% figure measures P&L impact of GenAI pilots, based on ~300 public deployments plus interviews; it has been widely reported (Fortune, Financial Express) and interpreted as "95% of pilots fail".
- FMI/PlanGrid rework dollar figures rest on an assumption that rework is 5% of construction spending; useful for scale, not measured national totals.
- Cyberhaven, LayerX and Corvus are security vendors with a commercial interest in the size of the problem; their telemetry is large-scale but definitions of "sensitive" are theirs.
- Microsoft/CMU and MIT Media Lab studies are self-reported/correlational and small-sample (319 workers; 54 participants); they indicate direction, not causal effect sizes.
- Vendor surveys (Bluebeam, Dodge/CMiC, Autodesk, Procore) survey their customer bases; RICS is professional-body research with a UK skew (~48% of respondents).
- The Writer, Thomson Reuters, Pacific AI and LinkedIn "champion" statistics are survey self-reports: they show correlation between strategy/training maturity and outcomes, not audited causal effects. Organizations that formalize an AI strategy may differ from those that do not in other ways.
- IBM's breach statistics describe the breached population (600 organizations) and measure what breached organizations lacked, not the effect of adopting controls; the $1.9 million security-automation saving is a comparison within the breached sample.
- The Cloud Security Alliance's 89% drop in unauthorized use comes from Unseen Security vendor research whose underlying methodology is not published; treat as indicative, not measured.
- NCCER's training comparison is observational (companies that choose to train differ from those that do not), and it measures craft training, not AI training; the productivity-estimate and rework figures are company-reported.
- Dell'Acqua et al. is the strongest direct evidence (randomized, preregistered) but studies consultants, not construction professionals, and effect sizes for GPT-4-era models may not transfer to newer models.
- AWS productivity figures (39% EU / 47% US) are employer expectations of gains from AI-skilled staff, not measured outcomes.
