# Winner of the Inaugural Asia Pacific Edition of the World's Largest Competition for Construction Start-ups

> Source: https://www1.bca.gov.sg/resources/newsroom/winner-of-the-inaugural-asia-pacific-edition-of-the-world-s-largest-competition-for-construction-start-ups/
> Collected: 2026-11-12
> Published: 2026-09-02
> Publisher: Building and Construction Authority (BCA), Singapore

[Media release from BCA, 2 September 2026, issued at the ConTech Summit during International Built Environment Week (IBEW) 2026. Reproduced below with the sections relevant to the five AI use cases, followed by notes on what the release does and does not establish.]

## Headline

Civils.ai was crowned **Gold Winner** of the inaugural APAC Pitch Day of the **Construction Startup Competition (CSC)**, selected by a jury of global built environment leaders from five APAC Track winners that emerged from **over 243 APAC applicants**.

The CSC is in its **10th edition**; BCA describes it as "widely regarded as the most prestigious global contest in the Construction Technology (ConTech) space". The APAC Pitch Day was held at the ConTech Summit during IBEW 2026.

## The five APAC Track winners

BCA's descriptions, verbatim:

1. **Arbel.ai** — "turns drone footage into searchable, conversational site intelligence, giving owners and EPCs planned-vs-actual visibility on progress, quality, and safety with zero manual reporting."
2. **Civils.ai** — "an AI co-pilot for civil engineers that extracts and cross-checks data from drawings, contracts, and geotechnical reports, cutting takeoff time by up to 90%."
3. **ConcreteAI** — "uses Internet of Thing (IoT) sensors that track in-situ concrete strength and temperature in real time, cutting casting-cycle waiting times by over 30% and reducing reliance on cube testing."
4. **Bton.io** — "produces patented low-clinker mix technology cuts concrete's CO2 footprint by up to 70%, with carbon-sinking additives pushing it climate-positive."
5. **LIGHTYX** — "uses a laser-projection system that puts BIM plans directly onto floors, walls, and ceilings at sub-2mm accuracy, replacing chalk lines and tape measures for layout crews."

Six APAC Track winners were selected; five accepted the invitation to pitch. The sixth was **Dig Robotics**.

## Civils.ai: what this source establishes

The release is the strongest **independent** description of Civils.ai's capability available in this collection, because it is written by a government agency rather than the vendor:

- It is an **AI co-pilot for civil engineers**
- It **extracts and cross-checks data from drawings, contracts and geotechnical reports**
- It cuts **takeoff time by up to 90%**

Three consequences follow for how the tool is classified:

1. **It belongs to BCA use case 3 (tender, cost and contract management), not use case 1 (knowledge management).** Takeoff measurement and document cross-checking are quantity and cost workflows. They do not create a queryable knowledge base over codes, standards and project documentation, and they do not answer compliance questions in natural language.
2. **The 90% is a takeoff-time figure, not a general productivity figure.** BCA describes it as "cutting takeoff time by up to 90%"; the vendor's own site describes the same capability as "reducing manual effort by up to 90%". Both refer to the takeoff task.
3. **Nothing in the release is a measured deployment result.** It reports a competition outcome and a vendor-supplied capability description. No customer is named, no baseline is given, and the 90% is not independently measured by BCA.

## ConTech investment and ecosystem context

- Global ConTech investment recorded an all-time high of **USD 6.57 billion in 2025**
- APAC accounted for **almost 20%** of global ConTech investment in 2025
- The 2026 CSC received a record **nearly 800 submissions from 64 countries**, a **40% increase** on 2025
- **243** of those applications came from APAC built environment startups
- Applications from Asia and Oceania rose **181%** year on year
- Civils.ai's prize: **EUR 10,000** cash sponsored by Meta, entry to the CSC top 30 with a chance to compete in the Global Finals, and access to the competition's investor network
- CSC finalist startups have collectively secured **over US$1 billion** in investment

Sources for the investment figures, per the release: Cemex Ventures Top 50 Startups Report 2025, https://www.cemexventures.com/top-50/

## Jury and organisers

The jury comprised Cemex Ventures, Caterpillar, Dysruptek by Haskell, Ferrovial, Hilti, VINCI Group's Leonard, NOVA by Saint-Gobain, Trimble, Zacua Ventures and BCA. BCA is both an organiser and a jury member.

Minister of State for Foreign Affairs and National Development Alvin Tan, in his closing address, framed Singapore's role as providing a "test track and commercial runway": "Though their tech is good, moving beyond small pilot trials to commercial scale is tough. You need capital, you need real-world construction sites to test your machines, and you need actual buyers who are ready to adopt them."

## What the release does not say

- No deployment data, no customer reference, no before/after measurement
- No methodology for the 90% takeoff figure
- No indication of how many Singapore projects, if any, use Civils.ai
- The startup descriptions are vendor-supplied and reproduced by BCA; they are a capability claim, not an evaluation

## Note on the earlier JTC reference

The JTC-related figures previously associated with Civils.ai in this collection (**38 users**, **2,842 AI prompts**, **5–15 minutes saved per prompt**, **528+ hours**, **97% takeoff accuracy**) come from the vendor's own homepage, https://www.civils.ai/, which states: "We help firms like JTC automate quantities measurements from drawings and run AI checks across their projects — with ROI verified over 12 months of Civils.ai usage across a team of 38 users."

There is **no published case study, methodology note or audit** behind those figures. The "ROI verified over 12 months" phrasing is the vendor's own. The figures are usable only as a self-reported, unaudited claim, and they belong to the takeoff and drawing-check workflow (use case 3), not to knowledge management (use case 1).
